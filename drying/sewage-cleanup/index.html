Backed-up drains and dirty water in Alpharetta houses

Sewage Backup Cleanup in Alpharetta, GA

Is dark water rising around a floor drain, or does the whole house smell wrong after a toilet backed up? Sewage in an Alpharetta house is a health matter first and a cleanup second. Once everyone is clear of it, call (678) 929-5373 and ask about a time to look at what the water reached.

Stay clear first, clean up second

  1. At the start.

    Floodwater can carry waste and other germs, according to the CDC, and its flooded-home page warns that a flooded house may be contaminated with sewage. Children stay out of it.

  2. The first day.

    Washing with soap and clean water after contact, and throwing out food that may have touched the water, are both on the CDC's list.

  3. The days after.

    EPA's mold cleanup page points to a professional experienced in cleaning buildings damaged by sewage or other contaminated water. From the CDC: items that cannot be disinfected, rugs and drywall among them, go in the trash.

Get out and get help if

  1. Sewage across the floor: do not touch it. Children and pets go to a different room. North Fulton sewer emergencies go to 404-612-3061; Forsyth County Water and Sewer is 770-781-2160.
  2. Outlets, cords or a panel in contact with the water: stay out of it. If the switch is on the far side of the wet floor, hand the job to 911 or the electric utility.
  3. Gas smell in the house: get outside first and leave the switches untouched. Atlanta Gas Light takes calls on 877.427.4321, and 911 works too.
  4. Below an overflowing upstairs fixture, a ceiling that sags or drips keeps people out of the room, and 911 is the call if it begins to give way.

Where the water usually comes from

  • A clog in the house's own sewer line that pushes water up the lowest drain
  • A problem in the public sewer main that pushes flow back toward houses
  • Tree roots in an older sewer line
  • Heavy rain that overloads drains
  • Things flushed or rinsed down a drain that catch and clog

What can be cleaned and what goes out

When a material cannot be disinfected, the CDC says it goes out; rugs, cloth, drywall and wall coverings are its examples. Hard surfaces and soft ones differ, and the contractor decides which is which after looking, not over the phone. EPA's note that sewage damage needs someone experienced is one more reason to leave the cleanup to a professional.

Looks you can take from dry ground

Stay with looks that feel comfortable from dry ground. Leave panels, wires, standing water near cords and sewage alone.

  1. Which drains are involved? Note from the doorway whether it is the floor drain, the tub or a toilet, and stop adding water: no flushing, no running taps.

  2. Photograph the water line and the affected rooms from dry ground.

  3. After any contact, the CDC advises washing with soap and clean water.

  4. Set aside food, bottles and pet dishes that were in the room. The CDC says to throw away food that may have touched the water.

Questions to settle before the cleanup starts

When you call (678) 929-5373, say which drains backed up.

Ask who clears the blockage: a plumber for the line from the house, the utility for the public main. Ask what happens to soaked flooring and walls, and how the cleanup is kept apart from the rest of the house.

Who clears the main

A plumber or the water utility can say whether the clog is inside the house's own line or out in the public main. Johns Creek's public works page gives 404-612-3061 for sewer problems, with press 1 for an emergency after hours, and Roswell's water page puts every sewer line with Fulton County. The city-desk page lists the rest.
