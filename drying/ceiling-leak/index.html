Ceiling and Wall Leaks in Alpharetta, GA

Has the stain overhead grown since last week, or did it only just catch your eye? In an Alpharetta house the water usually starts one floor up or on the roof. If the edge has crept past your pencil line, call (678) 929-5373 and ask about a time to find where it comes from.

A stain, a bulge, a drip

  1. A faint stain.

    Pencil in its edge and date it. EPA's mold cleanup guide asks that plumbing leaks and water problems of any kind be repaired as soon as possible, and that everything wet be dried completely.

  2. A bulge or a sag.

    A sagging ceiling has taken on water, says the American Red Cross, and that makes it heavy and dangerous. Everyone stays out from under it.

  3. Once the source is found and the area is dry. Mississippi State University Extension suggests giving walls at least four to six weeks to dry completely, and EPA's flood page gives a moisture-meter reading of 15 percent or less as the mark for refinishing.

Get out and get help if

  1. Drywall overhead that sags, bulges or drips: nobody stands beneath it. Move children and pets out of the room, and use 911 at the first sign it is coming down.
  2. Water reaching a light fixture, fan or outlet: leave the switches alone. With a wet floor between you and the main switch, 911 or the electric utility takes over.
  3. Smoke, a burnt smell or a flicker from a ceiling fixture: leave the house, then use 911 from outside.
  4. A whiff of gas: step outside, leave every switch alone, and from the yard phone Atlanta Gas Light, 877.427.4321, or 911.

Where the water usually comes from

  • Roof water that soaks through attic insulation and shows up on the ceiling below
  • A supply or drain line running above the ceiling, usually over a bathroom or kitchen
  • A tub, washer or water heater upstairs that leaks or overflows
  • Wind-driven rain forced past a worn window or door seal into the wall
  • An air handler's condensate line that clogs and spills into the ceiling

Patch the section, or open more of the ceiling

Firm, dry drywall with a stain on it may need nothing beyond a stopped leak and confirmation that it has dried. Drywall that has gone soft, bulged or sagged is another matter, and the contractor decides whether a section comes down. Insulation above can stay wet where no one sees it. Heavy and dangerous is how the Red Cross describes a wet, sagging ceiling, so bringing it down is work for crews with the equipment.

Looks you can take from dry ground

Stay with looks that feel comfortable from dry ground. Leave panels, wires, standing water near cords and sewage alone.

  1. Look at the stain from a few feet back for a bulge. Never poke or press on a stained ceiling.

  2. Draw a pencil line around the stain's edge and date it; if the mark creeps past the line, it is still growing.

  3. Go to the room above and check for a damp floor, a fixture left running, or a wet spot under a tub or sink.

  4. Shift furniture out from beneath the spot only when the floor under it is dry and the ceiling is holding its shape.

Tracing the water uphill

Call (678) 929-5373 and give the date the stain first appeared.

Ask how the contractor follows a leak from the stain back to its source, what gets checked in the attic or the room above, and when a roofer or plumber needs to join in.

Drywall repair and the town desk

Milton's residential page lists painting, floor coverings, cabinets and counter tops as jobs that generally need no permit, and it says nothing about ceilings or drywall. Alpharetta's trade permits page speaks to subcontractors and points to an online portal. City Inspections, 678-297-6080, can say what applies to your address, and the city-desk page covers the other towns.
