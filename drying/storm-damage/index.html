Roof leaks and wind-driven rain, Alpharetta area

Storm and Roof Leak Water Damage, Alpharetta, GA

Rain came in, but where? In an Alpharetta house, storm water often turns up in a room well away from the roof or window that let it in. Once the storm has passed, call (678) 929-5373 and say where the first drip showed.

Before the storm, during it, and after

  1. Before.

    A UGA Extension field report puts the Atlantic hurricane season at June 1 through November 30, and its climatologist warned that inland flooding is a concern statewide. UGA's home checklist says to look for roof and attic leaks and to send gutter water two to three feet from the house.

  2. During.

    The National Weather Service in Peachtree City says flash floods usually follow heavy rain by six hours or less, and it repeats one rule: turn around, don't drown. Never try to cross a flooded road.

  3. After.

    In its 2020 tips for Hurricane Zeta, the office of Georgia's insurance commissioner advised writing down and photographing the damage, keeping receipts and leaving significant repairs to others. For scale, NWS radar estimates for Hurricane Helene averaged 9 to 11 inches over Fulton County and 7 to 9 inches over Forsyth County.

Get out and get help if

  1. A line down, or a wire resting on a tree or fence: keep well back and use 911, or Georgia Power at 1-888-891-0938.
  2. After a roof leak, a ceiling that sags, bulges or drips stays off limits; use 911 if it begins to fall.
  3. Generators and pumps that burn gasoline run outside only, with 20 feet between them and any door, window or vent. A carbon monoxide alarm that goes off means everyone leaves and 911 is next.
  4. Gas odor after storm damage: go outdoors, touch no switch, then use 911, or call the gas utility, Atlanta Gas Light, on its emergency number, 877.427.4321.

Where the water usually comes from

  • Wind-lifted or damaged roof covering that lets rain reach the decking
  • Clogged gutters that send water behind the fascia and into the wall
  • A window or door that lets wind-driven rain inside
  • A fallen limb or tree that opened the roof
  • Yard water that runs toward a low door or window after hard rain

Roof first, then the drying

The roof or window that let the water in needs its own repair, and the contractor looking at the wet rooms can say which other trade is needed. Insulation and drywall under a leak can stay wet where no one can see them, and EPA's flood page says drying usually takes several days to weeks. Which materials dry where they sit and which come out is the contractor's call after reading them.

Looks you can take from dry ground

Stay with looks that feel comfortable from dry ground. Leave panels, wires, standing water near cords and sewage alone.

  1. Stay off ladders and wet roofs. From the yard, look at the roof edge, the gutters and the chimney for anything loose or missing.

  2. Inside, look at the ceilings under the roofline and around the attic hatch for stains, drips or dark patches.

  3. Put a pail under any drip and jot down the time it began.

  4. Photograph each wet spot and the outside of the house before anything is moved.

Roof work and the town desk

Johns Creek can issue a reroof permit over the counter, its building FAQs say. Alpharetta's storm tree page treats emergency tree removals case by case, and Johns Creek sends a tree down in the road or a downed power line to 911. The city-desk page gives each town's number.
