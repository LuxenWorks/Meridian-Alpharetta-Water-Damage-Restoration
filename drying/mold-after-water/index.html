Musty rooms and dark spots after a leak, Alpharetta

Mold After Water Damage in Alpharetta, GA

Does a closet smell musty, or has a speckle shown up near the baseboard since the leak? Mold needs moisture to grow, and EPA says the water problem comes first. When the smell is still there a day after the surface dries, call __PHONE_DISPLAY__ and ask about a time for a moisture check.

How mold follows water

  1. The first day or two.

    None of the many types of mold will grow without water or moisture, says EPA's guide to mold and moisture, which also sets a drying target of 24 to 48 hours for water-damaged areas and items.

  2. Beyond a day.

    EPA's flood-cleanup page says mold can take hold in wood, drywall, carpet and furniture left damp for over 24 hours.

  3. Days to weeks.

    Drying usually takes several days to weeks, the same page says, and EPA's mold cleanup page adds that mold already present is dealt with soon, since it damages what it grows on.

  4. Before repainting.

    Mississippi State University Extension suggests at least four to six weeks for walls to dry completely.

  5. Renters: EPA's guide asks tenants to tell the building owner, manager or superintendent about leaks and moisture problems at once, so the first call is the landlord's.

Get out and get help if

  1. Damp room with wet outlets or standing water: keep to dry ground, and leave a breaker or switch beyond the wet floor to 911 or the electric utility.
  2. Smell of gas in a damp room: leave the building, touch no switch, and reach Atlanta Gas Light's emergency line at 877.427.4321, or use 911.
  3. A ceiling over a musty room that sags, bulges or drips: clear the room of people and pets, and use 911 if it starts to fall.
  4. Dark, foul water on the floor from a drain: keep hands off, children and pets out. Sewer calls: Fulton County 404-612-3061, Forsyth County Water and Sewer 770-781-2160.

Where the water usually comes from

  • A leak that was stopped but never dried
  • Carpet or padding that stayed wet after a spill
  • Drywall or insulation that stayed damp inside a wall
  • A damp crawl space with little airflow
  • A slow drip under a sink or behind an appliance

Where the moisture still sits

Mold is a symptom of wet materials, so the contractor's first question is how wet things still are. A moisture meter answers it, and EPA's flood page names 15 percent or less as the mark for refinishing. Drywall, carpet padding and insulation that stayed wet are the usual suspects. EPA's mold cleanup guide has its own section on small patches and on what to wear; read the do-it-yourself steps there.

Looks you can take from dry ground

Stay with looks that feel comfortable from dry ground. Leave panels, wires, standing water near cords and sewage alone.

  1. With a flashlight, look along the lowest two feet of the walls, under sinks and behind furniture on outside walls.

  2. Note where the musty smell is strongest and write the date, so a change shows over time.

  3. Photograph any spots with a tape measure beside them for scale.

  4. Look for a source that is still active: a drip under a sink, a damp crawl space or a stain that keeps growing.

What to ask about the moisture

Call __PHONE_DISPLAY__ and give the date of the original leak.

Ask what the contractor uses to read moisture, which rooms get read and how the readings are written down. EPA says it does not list flood cleanup contractors or vouch for any, so ask which Georgia license fits the job and see the hiring page for how to check it.

Which Georgia license fits mold work

Georgia's contractor pages do not say whether drying or mold work falls under any state license. Ask the contractor which license applies, then have the Secretary of State's licensing division confirm it. Details are on the hiring and city-desk page.
