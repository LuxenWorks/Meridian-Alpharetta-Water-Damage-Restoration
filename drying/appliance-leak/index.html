Washers, dishwashers and water heaters, Alpharetta area

Appliance and Water Heater Leaks, Alpharetta, GA

Found a damp towel behind the washer, or a puddle by the water heater? In an Alpharetta house, a leaking appliance spreads under cabinets and flooring before the surface looks wet. Pick up the phone and dial (678) 929-5373 and describe where the puddle began.

A slow drip, a puddle, a soaked floor

  1. A slow drip.

    UGA Extension's checklist asks owners to check washing machine hoses, replacing any that leak, and to test the water heater's relief valve. EPA's WaterSense leak guide estimates that about 20 percent of toilets leak.

  2. A puddle.

    The same EPA guide names a washing machine hose and a failed water heater as examples of a catastrophic leak. A puddle that returns after being wiped away is the cue to phone.

  3. A soaked floor.

    When water has reached the flooring, EPA's mold guide sets a drying target of 24 to 48 hours, and its flood page says drying typically takes several days to weeks.

Get out and get help if

  1. Puddled water at the water heater, an outlet or a cord: stay out of it, and if the power switch is beyond the puddle, leave that to 911 or the electric utility.
  2. Gas smell near a water heater or dryer: go outside, skip the switches, then use 911 or the gas utility's emergency number, 877.427.4321.
  3. Hot water or steam jetting from a tank valve: step back and keep kids and pets clear. Use 911 if anyone is scalded.
  4. A ceiling under the leak that sags or drips: no one stands beneath it, and 911 is the call if it begins to drop.

Where the water usually comes from

  • A washer hose that cracks or slips off its fitting
  • A dishwasher, ice maker or refrigerator line that drips behind the unit
  • A water heater tank or fitting that corrodes and weeps
  • A relief valve that opens and discharges onto the floor
  • A toilet or sink supply that seeps under the cabinet

Repair the machine, or replace it

Whether a hose, a valve or a whole water heater is repaired or replaced is a plumber's decision. The contractor who takes your call deals with the water that escaped, and both jobs can go on at the same time. On the wet side, the contractor looks under the machine, inside the cabinet or wall behind it and at the flooring beneath, then reads how wet each one is.

Looks you can take from dry ground

Stay with looks that feel comfortable from dry ground. Leave panels, wires, standing water near cords and sewage alone.

  1. Look at the hoses behind the washer for cracks, bulges or drips, and at the shutoff valves for crust or moisture.

  2. Press a dry paper towel against the floor beside the machine. A damp towel means the leak is recent.

  3. Look under the water heater and along the nearby overhead pipes for wet spots or rust marks. UGA's checklist includes testing the relief valve, a job for a plumber.

  4. Photograph the puddle, the appliance's label and the damp flooring.

Sorting out whose job is what

Call (678) 929-5373 when the floor under the machine has stayed damp.

Ask whether the contractor handles the water side while a plumber or an appliance technician handles the machine. Ask what readings get taken under cabinets and flooring, and how long a damp subfloor stays under watch.

Swapping a water heater

Johns Creek puts water heater change-outs on its list of over-the-counter permits, and Milton lists moving or adding plumbing service or appliances as permit jobs. Alpharetta's pages cover no rule for a swap, so City Inspections at 678-297-6080 can answer. See the city-desk page for the other towns.
