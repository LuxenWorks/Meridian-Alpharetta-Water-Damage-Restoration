Soaked carpet and floors, Alpharetta to Roswell

Water Extraction and Drying in Alpharetta, GA

Does the carpet squelch with every step, or does a puddle keep returning after the mop? In an Alpharetta house, standing water works into the padding, the baseboards and the lower wall before the surface shows it. If the floor still feels wet tomorrow morning, call __PHONE_DISPLAY__ and ask about a time for someone to measure how far it went.

How the first days tend to go

  1. The first hour.

    If stopping the source is safe, do it, then photograph the room from dry ground. EPA's mold guide allows 24 to 48 hours for water-damaged areas and belongings to dry, so the clock is already running.

  2. The first day.

    Mold can take hold in wood, drywall, carpet and furniture once they have stayed wet past 24 hours, says EPA's flood-cleanup page; a soaked room qualifies.

  3. Days to weeks.

    The same page says complete drying usually takes several days to weeks. CDC lists fans, air conditioning units and dehumidifiers as drying tools, though EPA says an HVAC system the flood reached stays out of the drying.

  4. Before anything is closed up.

    EPA's flood page names a moisture-meter reading of 15 percent or less as the mark for refinishing, and Mississippi State University Extension's wall guide sets four to six weeks as the minimum before repainting.

Get out and get help if

  1. Wet outlets, cords or a panel near the soaked floor: stay on dry ground and touch no switch. A main switch you cannot reach dry is for 911 or the electric utility to handle.
  2. Sparks, a hum or a scorched smell at an outlet or fixture: go outside, then use 911.
  3. Gas smell: head outdoors at once, touch no switch or flame, and from well clear use the gas utility's emergency number, 877.427.4321, or 911.
  4. Water that rose through a drain or toilet: stay off it, and keep kids and pets elsewhere. Sewer calls: Fulton County 404-612-3061, Forsyth County Water and Sewer 770-781-2160.

Where the water usually comes from

  • A supply line, fitting or drain joint that fails faster than the floor drain can carry the flow
  • A washer, dishwasher or water heater that gives out and floods across the floor
  • A tub, sink or toilet that runs on or overflows while nobody is in the room
  • Heavy rain pushed in through a door, a window well or a foundation wall
  • A drain that backs up into the lowest room of the house

Dry it where it lies, or take it out

The contractor makes this call room by room, meter in hand. A clean spill caught early is one case; water that carried germs is another. CDC puts drywall, rugs, cloth and wall coverings on its throw-away list when they cannot be disinfected, and Mississippi State University Extension's wall guide says insulation soaked inside a wall usually gets removed and replaced. Expect the answers to differ from one room to the next.

Looks you can take from dry ground

Stay with looks that feel comfortable from dry ground. Leave panels, wires, standing water near cords and sewage alone.

  1. From a dry doorway, judge how far the water has crept and whether it is still moving, then photograph the floor, the baseboards and the base of the wall.

  2. Work out where the water began, whether pipe, appliance, window or drain, and note the time you first spotted it.

  3. Locate the main water shutoff and confirm you could reach it without wading; UGA Extension advises knowing where it is in advance.

  4. Check the ceiling in any room under an upstairs leak; a stain, bulge or drip there means staying out from under it.

Questions worth putting to the contractor

Keep the answers with a note of the address, the wet rooms and how long the water has sat. With that note in hand, call __PHONE_DISPLAY__.

Ask the town desk before walls open

None of the city pages for Alpharetta, Milton, Johns Creek, Roswell or Cumming describes a rule aimed at drying or at tearing out materials after water damage. Each town's desk can say whether one applies to your address, and the hiring and city-desk page lists the desks.
