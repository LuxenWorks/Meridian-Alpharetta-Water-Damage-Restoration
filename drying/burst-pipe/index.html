Burst Pipe Water Damage in Alpharetta, GA

Is something hissing inside a wall, or is a stain growing under an upstairs bathroom? A burst pipe in an Alpharetta house sends water through the wall cavity, across the floor and down into the room below. Call __PHONE_DISPLAY__, name the wall or ceiling that is wet, and ask about a time for a look.

From the split to the rebuild

  1. The first minutes.

    UGA Extension's field report urges homeowners to know where the main water cutoff is. Gwinnett County's freezing-weather page tells owners with frozen pipes to close the main valve and let the pipes thaw, then bring in a plumber to assess the damage.

  2. The first days.

    A plumber repairs the pipe; drying what it soaked is a separate job. EPA's mold guide wants wet areas dry in 24 to 48 hours.

  3. The weeks.

    Mississippi State University Extension warns that a wet wall cavity can need weeks or even months to dry, and puts the minimum wait before repainting at four to six weeks.

Get out and get help if

  1. Water showing at a ceiling light, fan or outlet: step back from the fixture and leave the switches alone. Power you cannot reach dry is for 911 or the electric utility.
  2. A ceiling that bulges, sags or drips is carrying a load of water and can drop: clear the room, pets included, and use 911 if it starts to fall.
  3. Gas odor around the water heater or furnace: leave first, then use 911 or the gas utility's emergency number, 877.427.4321, from a safe distance.
  4. Water gushing from a line outside, near the street or the meter box: Fulton County lists 770-640-3040 for drinking water emergencies in North Fulton.

Where the water usually comes from

  • A pipe that froze, expanded and split on a cold night
  • A worn pipe or joint that gives way under everyday water pressure
  • A hose or fitting at a washer, sink or toilet that works loose
  • A fastener driven into a line during earlier work inside the wall
  • Aging pipe that thins out where it bends or joins

Repair the pipe, then dry the wall

A plumber repairs or replaces the split section. The contractor who answers your call examines what the water reached: the wall cavity, the insulation, the subfloor and the ceiling below. Mississippi State University Extension's wall guide says soaked insulation usually has to come out and be replaced, and that drywall which sat under water is replaced too. A split supply line may not go that far, and the contractor decides after looking.

Looks you can take from dry ground

Stay with looks that feel comfortable from dry ground. Leave panels, wires, standing water near cords and sewage alone.

  1. If you can reach the main water cutoff from dry ground, locate it now; UGA Extension advises knowing where it is ahead of time.

  2. With every tap shut, read the water meter and see whether the dial still turns. A turning dial means water is still escaping the pipes.

  3. Photograph the split, the soaked wall and every stain before anything is opened or moved.

  4. Check the rooms above and below the pipe for a ceiling stain that spreads.

What to tell the contractor, and what to ask

Once the main water is off and the photographs are taken, call __PHONE_DISPLAY__.

Name the wall or ceiling that is wet and say where you found the split. Ask whether a plumber repairs the pipe first, how hidden wet spots inside the wall get found, and which rooms below get checked.

Pipe work and paperwork

Water heater change-outs are among the permits Johns Creek issues over the counter, its building FAQs say, while Milton's residential page lists moving or adding plumbing service as a permit job. Alpharetta's pages say nothing about water damage repair, so City Inspections at 678-297-6080 is the number to ask. The city-desk page covers the remaining towns.
