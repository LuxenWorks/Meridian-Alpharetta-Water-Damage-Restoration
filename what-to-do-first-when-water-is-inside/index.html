What to do first when water is inside the house, in six checks

Water has come inside and every minute feels loud. These six checks run in order: electricity, gas, dark water, a sagging ceiling, the supply valve, then the first day of drying, each with the agency behind it.

  1. 1

    If water is standing near outlets, cords or the panel

    CDC's rule for a flooded home is blunt: no switching power on or off, and no electric tools or appliances, while you stand in water. When the only route to the main switch runs through standing water, CDC says an electrician turns it off. Sparks, humming or a burnt smell at a fixture mean leaving the house and using 911 from outside. A line down in the yard or street is a different call: Georgia Power lists 1-888-891-0938 or 911 and says never to go near it.

  2. 2

    If you smell gas

    Atlanta Gas Light's emergency page tells people to leave the area immediately, move a safe distance away and avoid anything that could ignite the gas, cell phones, cigarettes and matches among them. Once you are clear, 877.427.4321 reaches Atlanta Gas Light's emergency line, and 911 also works; stay away until the gas company or emergency crews say it is safe to return. A smell of gas near a water heater, furnace or range that stood in water gets the same response.

  3. 3

    If the water is dark, smells of drains, or came up through a toilet or floor drain

    Human waste and other germs can ride in floodwater, the CDC warns, and it wants children kept out of it and any skin that touched it washed with soap and clean water as soon as possible. Food that may have touched the water is thrown away, even when it looks and smells fine. For damage by sewage or any other contaminated water, EPA's mold guide points to a professional experienced in cleaning buildings damaged that way. For sewer trouble in North Fulton the county's number is 404-612-3061, and Forsyth County Water and Sewer's after-hours line is 770-781-2160.

  4. 4

    If a ceiling sags, bulges or drips

    A sagging ceiling has taken on water, the American Red Cross explains, which makes it heavy and dangerous. Nobody stands under it, pets leave too, and 911 is the call if it starts to come down. Do not poke or press on a bulge. A stain without a bulge can wait for a pencil trace and a date, the check described on the ceiling and wall leaks page.

  5. 5

    If the water is clean and the source is a pipe, tank or hose

    Stopping the supply comes before any cleanup. UGA Extension tells owners to know where the main water cutoff is, and Gwinnett County's water department names the main shut off valve as the first move for frozen pipes. After that, write down and photograph the damage. Georgia's insurance office advises documenting it, notifying the insurance company and saving receipts, and says not to attempt significant repairs.

  6. 6

    Once the water has stopped

    Every hour of dampness counts. EPA's guide to mold and moisture wants water-damaged areas and items dry in 24 to 48 hours, and its flood-cleanup page names wood, drywall, carpet and furniture as the materials that can grow mold after more than 24 hours wet. CDC lists fans, air conditioning units and dehumidifiers for drying, while EPA keeps any HVAC system the water reached out of the drying. Fully dry is a longer road: EPA gives several days to weeks, and Mississippi State University Extension puts the wait before repainting at four to six weeks or more.

  7. 7

    Which page comes next, and when is it time to call?

    If carpet or flooring stayed wet past the first day, water extraction and drying explains what a contractor reads and removes. If the water came from a line inside a wall, burst pipe water damage lists the questions to settle first.
