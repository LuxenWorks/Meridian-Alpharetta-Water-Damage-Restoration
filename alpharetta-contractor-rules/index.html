Before a wall is opened: Georgia license and city permit rules

Before drywall comes down, two questions are worth settling: whether the job calls for a Georgia contractor license, and whether the town wants a permit. The answers below come from the state and city pages themselves, and the gaps in those pages are marked as gaps. Call (678) 929-5373 to talk through a particular house.

01

Does the job call for a Georgia contractor license?

The Secretary of State's contractor FAQ ties the answer to O.C.G.A. 43-41-2(4), which defines a contractor as anyone who, for compensation, performs or manages construction for an owner; that person needs a license unless the chapter exempts the work. Among the exemptions the FAQ lists are some specialty trades and work costing under $2,500. It names three license types: Residential-Basic, for detached one- and two-family homes and one-family townhouses of up to 3 stories; Residential-Light Commercial, which adds multi-family and light commercial buildings; and General Contractor. O.C.G.A. 43-41-17 adds that no person or business may engage in residential or commercial general contracting without a current, valid license from the division, and it lists exemptions of its own.

02

What the state pages do not say

Neither the FAQ nor the code section says in plain words whether drying, water extraction or mold work counts as construction under that license. The useful questions go to the contractor: which license fits the job, what is its number, and who holds it? The Secretary of State's licensing division can confirm the answer, and the state call center, 1-800-GEORGIA (1-800-436-7442), is open on weekdays between 8 a.m. and 5 p.m. Eastern. EPA's flood-cleanup page adds that the agency has no program for approving flood cleanup contractors and keeps no list of them.

03

Looking up a name

The Secretary of State's lookup at verify.sos.ga.gov searches by profession, license type, number or name, and describes itself as intended for individual verification only. Its opening page does not name the boards it covers, so the Profession and License Type menus are where to see whether contractor entries are included. If a name does not turn up, ask the contractor for the number and the issuing board, then check again.

04

Alpharetta: calling in an inspection

To call in an inspection in Alpharetta, phone the City Inspections Division at 678-297-6080 on the day it is wanted, from 7:30 until 8:30 a.m. The trade permits page says an online portal lets subcontractors apply for permits and track permits and inspections; it states no license or registration rule. Neither that page nor the inspections page mentions repairs after water damage. Alpharetta also requires every business operating in the city to hold a current Occupational Tax Certificate, its name for a business license, from the first day of business. The page does not say whether that covers a contractor based elsewhere.

05

Milton: the CityView portal

Milton's Community Development department issues permits for all construction activity, and its pages say permits for building and renovation go through the CityView Web Portal. The residential page lists moving or adding plumbing, adding or replacing HVAC equipment and finishing a basement, attic or bonus room as permit jobs, and calls painting, floor coverings, cabinets and counter tops generally not required. Drywall and water damage repair are not addressed. City Hall's number is 678-242-2500, and the Economic Development line that Milton's start-a-business page gives for business license questions is 678-242-2534.

06

Johns Creek: what goes over the counter

Johns Creek's building FAQs say a permit is valid for 180 days and that some permits, among them water heater change-outs, service meter upgrades, reroofs and siding, can be issued over the counter. A contractor who applies shows the city a copy of the business license and, where state law calls for one, a State License. The Building Division's number is 678-512-3250, and the Revenue Department listed on the business resources page is at 678-512-3242. The city's pages do not say which water damage repairs need a permit.

07

Roswell: the Permitting & Licensing HUB

Roswell routes building, planning, fire, tree and other permits through its online HUB, and business license renewals use a HUB account too. The city's permitting page says Georgia law requires contractors who hold a license to get the needed building permits before beginning work, and it suggests that owners confirm whether a permit is needed before work starts. An owner who hires a contractor generally does not need an account, since the contractor applies under their own name and license type. City Hall, (770) 641-3727, answers questions on weekdays between 8 a.m. and 5 p.m. Water damage repair is not mentioned.

08

Cumming and Forsyth County: two desks

Inside Cumming, Planning & Zoning at City Hall, 770-781-2024, reviews plans, issues land disturbance and construction permits, inspects trade work and handles business licenses. Its pages link a Residential Subcontractor Affidavit and a Self-work Permit Affidavit. Forsyth County has its own Building and Licensing office, which accepts online plan submissions and inspection requests. The county gives its number as (770) 886-2772 on one page and 770-781-2114 in its contact directory, so confirm by phone before going in. The county's page also points readers to the Secretary of State for license rules and lookups.

09

What to ask before a wall is opened

Which Georgia license fits this job, and what is its number? Does the town want a permit for the repair, and who pulls it? If sewage is involved, who clears the main? Write the answers down before work starts.

Once the answers are written down, call (678) 929-5373 and ask about a time.
