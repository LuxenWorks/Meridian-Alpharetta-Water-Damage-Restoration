Wet-house calendar for Alpharetta: freezing nights, storms and hurricane season

A house does not stay dry by luck. This calendar walks the year around Alpharetta: the checklist walk for any month, nights below freezing, storm days, hurricane season, and what Helene and Delta did to the gauges.

  1. Any month: a walk with the checklist

    UGA Extension's home maintenance checklist (Circular 1082) works as a walk for any month. It asks owners to test the water-heater pressure and temperature-relief valve, to check washing machine hoses and replace any that leak, to confirm that gutters and downspouts discharge water 2 to 3 feet from the house, and to look for roof and attic leaks. For basements and crawl spaces it adds checking for signs of mold, moisture, water damage and leaks, and checking that the sump pump and floor drain work. EPA's WaterSense guide notes that about 20 percent of toilets leak, so the bathroom floor earns a look too.

  2. Nights below 32 degrees

    Gwinnett County's water department says water in home plumbing can expand and break pipes in freezing weather, and it advises covering outdoor water valves and spigots when temperatures dip below 32 degrees. UGA's Field Report adds insulating exposed pipes with tapes or foam, knowing where the main cutoff is, and disconnecting and draining hoses. If a pipe has frozen, Gwinnett's advice is to turn the water off at the main valve and let the pipes thaw. The burst pipe page covers what to read afterwards.

  3. Heavy rain, any season

    The National Weather Service office in Peachtree City says flash floods usually arrive six hours or less after heavy rainfall, and that nearly half of flood deaths are vehicle related. Its rule is short: turn around, don't drown. Water that gets into a house during a storm tends to show up as a ceiling stain, a wet attic or a damp basement wall, and the storm damage and flooded basement pages cover those.

  4. June 1 to November 30: hurricane season

    UGA's climatologist put the Atlantic hurricane season at June 1 through November 30 and warned that inland flooding is a concern statewide, from the coastal plain to the mountains. He also noted that heavy rains can flood the lowland Piedmont region, the part of Georgia that Alpharetta, Milton, Johns Creek, Cumming and Roswell sit in.

  5. 2024: Helene's rain

    In its county impact report for Hurricane Helene, the National Weather Service estimates by radar that Fulton County averaged 9 to 11 inches, with two flash flood emergencies, and that Forsyth County averaged 7 to 9, with the Big Creek and Dick Creek gauges reaching Minor Flood stage. The Atlanta station ended 2024 at 63.18 inches where a normal year brings 49.71, the 14th wettest year in its record, and 4.32 inches fell on September 26 alone.

  6. October 2020: Delta, then Zeta

    The remnants of Hurricane Delta reached north and central Georgia on October 10 and 11, 2020, and Atlanta set a daily rainfall record of 4.55 inches on the 10th; the old record was 3.42 inches, from 2018. The station finished 2020 at 66.99 inches against a normal of 49.71. On October 29, with Hurricane Zeta passing, Georgia's insurance commissioner's office issued tips: write down and photograph all damage, notify the insurance company immediately, do not attempt significant repairs, and save every receipt.

  7. Is it time to call after a storm or a freeze?

    A stain that appeared after a storm belongs with ceiling and wall leaks. A split pipe after a cold night belongs with burst pipe water damage.
