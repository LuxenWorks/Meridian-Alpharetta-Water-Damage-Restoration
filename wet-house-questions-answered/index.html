Wet-house questions answered: insurance, fans, food, walls, the main and renting

Six questions that come up once the water stops: whether insurance pays, whether fans and the air conditioner can dry a house, what to do with food, how long walls take, where the main shutoff is, and who calls when you rent.

Will my homeowners policy pay for flood damage?

UGA Extension's field report says most homeowner's policies do not cover flood damage, and Johns Creek's floodplain page says the same of a typical policy. Georgia's insurance office advises photographing damage, notifying the insurer, avoiding significant repairs and saving receipts, and lists a consumer line at 1-800-656-2298. What your own policy covers is a question for the policy and the insurer.

Can fans and the air conditioner dry the house?

The CDC's flood guidance lists fans, air conditioning units and dehumidifiers as drying tools. EPA makes one exception: when any part of the HVAC system was affected by the flood, it stays out of the drying. EPA's flood page also says drying usually takes several days to weeks, and it names a moisture-meter reading of 15 percent or less as the point for refinishing.

What happens to the food in the kitchen?

CDC says to throw away food that may have come in contact with flood or storm water, and adds that unsafe food can make you sick even if it looks, smells and tastes normal. For drinking, cooking and cleaning, CDC says to follow local officials' instructions on using water.

How long do the walls take to dry?

Mississippi State University Extension's wall guide sets four to six weeks as the minimum for walls to dry completely, and adds that the process can sometimes stretch to weeks or even months, depending on airflow, humidity and temperature. In most cases soaked insulation is removed and replaced, and drywall that was submerged is replaced. A contractor's moisture readings show better than the calendar whether a given wall has reached the mark.

Where is the main shutoff, and who covers the street side?

UGA Extension's winter-pipes article tells owners to know where the main water cutoff is before they need it, and Gwinnett County's water department names the main shut off valve as the first move for frozen pipes. For the street side, Fulton County lists 770-640-3040 for drinking water emergencies in North Fulton and 404-612-3061 for sewer, and Forsyth County Water and Sewer lists 770-781-2160 after hours.

I rent. Whose call comes first?

EPA's guide to mold and moisture asks a renter to tell the building owner, manager or superintendent right away about any plumbing leak or moisture problem. That call comes first, and the owner or manager can then decide who looks at the unit. Photograph the wet spots and write down the date and time before anything is moved.

Where does the call go when the answer is still unclear?

A stain on a ceiling or a wall goes with ceiling and wall leaks. Water that came up through a drain goes with sewage backup cleanup.
